\section{A Synthetic Hospital Benchmark with Known Drift}
\label{sec:synthetic-hospital}

Alongside the drifted real-life logs, we provide a \emph{fully synthetic} event log of a hospital patient
journey, from admission to discharge, in an undrifted \emph{baseline} version and a \emph{drifted} version
that carries a single, exactly known concept drift. Whereas editing a real log preserves realism but inherits
the natural variability of the source, a synthetic process is generated end-to-end and therefore comes with a
\emph{gold-standard} ground truth: activities, control-flow, durations, resources, arrivals, and case
attributes are all produced procedurally, so nothing is tied to a real organisation and the location and
nature of the drift are known by construction. The log is intended as a controlled benchmark for
concept-drift detection and, more broadly, for predictive process monitoring, business-process simulation,
and prescriptive process analytics.

\paragraph{Baseline (undrifted) generation.}
The process models an emergency/inpatient pathway. Each case starts with \texttt{Admission}, \texttt{Triage}
and \texttt{Initial Assessment}, optionally undergoes \texttt{Lab Tests}, \texttt{Imaging} and
\texttt{Specialist Consultation}, reaches a \texttt{Diagnosis}, and then follows either a surgical branch
(\texttt{Surgery}\,$\rightarrow$\,\texttt{Post-op Observation}) or a non-surgical one (\texttt{Treatment}),
with one or more \texttt{Medication Administration} events, before \texttt{Discharge} and
\texttt{Billing~\&~Payment}. The probability of imaging and of surgery is conditioned on the patient's
triage level, so that more urgent patients follow longer and more complex paths; the optional steps and the
branch choice, drawn per case, yield a rich set of trace variants over $13$ activities. Every perspective is
populated coherently: each activity is given a realistic \emph{execution time} sampled from a log-normal
distribution with a domain-plausible median (triage minutes, laboratory hours, post-operative observation
days), separated by \emph{waiting times}, while patient \emph{arrivals} follow a Poisson process. Resources
are a deliberate \emph{mix} of single-tasking units (individual doctors, nurses and imaging machines, which
serve one patient at a time) and multitasking shared units (laboratory teams, wards, admissions desk and
billing, which legitimately work concurrently). Finally, each case carries the attributes \texttt{Age},
\texttt{Department} and \texttt{TriageLevel}. The resulting baseline comprises $2{,}500$ cases and about
$23{,}000$ events; the whole generation is seeded and thus fully reproducible.

\paragraph{Drift injection.}
The drifted version is obtained from the baseline by injecting a single \emph{sudden} drift at a randomly
chosen change point, following the same methodology used for the real logs: the drift is confined to one
perspective, arrivals are never moved, and the change point is exact. In the released instance, a
\emph{time} drift is applied to \texttt{Lab Tests}, whose duration is multiplied by a factor of four for
every case after the change point, reflecting a laboratory backlog or a drop in capacity; the waiting times
between activities are kept constant, so only the affected activity and the events that follow it within each
case are shifted forward. As the target activity is executed by a multitasking (shared) unit, no resource
reassignment is required; had the target been a single-tasking resource, the conflict would be resolved by
reassigning the competing activity to another qualified resource or, failing that, by postponing it. The
effect is a clean, localised change: the median duration of \texttt{Lab Tests} rises from roughly two hours
and a half to about ten hours, the median length of stay increases accordingly, while the directly-follows
behaviour of the process is left unchanged---confirming that the drift affects the time perspective only.
Because the pre-change segment coincides with the undrifted baseline and the post-change segment differs from
it by exactly this drift, the pair provides a precise, self-contained ground truth for evaluating
drift-detection and process-analytics methods.
